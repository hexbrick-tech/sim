# Prologue — Origin and Position of SIM

## 1. Why SIM Exists

Semantic Isolation Method (SIM) began with a question about AI bias.

AI systems are useful because they can draw on large bodies of prior knowledge, learned patterns, optimization objectives, system design, and contextual information. The same properties that make them useful, however, can also introduce influences that did not originate from the person using them.

An answer may be plausible, coherent, and useful while still containing assumptions, priorities, interpretations, or biases supplied by the system rather than by the user.

The first instinct is often to remove such bias.

But this immediately creates another problem.

To remove bias, someone must decide what counts as bias. To define neutrality, someone must decide what counts as neutral. To correct an interpretation, someone must decide what the correct interpretation is.

Those decisions are themselves acts of observation and interpretation.

The attempt to eliminate bias can therefore introduce another bias while hiding the act that introduced it.

SIM emerged from this problem.

It does not begin by asking how observation can become perfectly neutral. It begins by asking how the act of observation itself can remain observable.

> **Observe the observer.**

This is the point from which SIM develops.

## 2. From Elimination to Observation

SIM does not treat bias as something that must always be removed before reasoning can proceed.

Instead, it treats bias, perspective, assumptions, interpretation, and uncertainty as things that may themselves be observed.

This changes the goal.

The goal is no longer to construct an observer with no perspective. The goal is to prevent a perspective from silently becoming the meaning of the observed subject.

A difference between observers is therefore not automatically an error. It may reveal different assumptions, contexts, boundaries, or interpretations.

An unknown is not automatically a defect that must be filled. It may represent a genuine absence of information.

An ambiguity is not automatically a failure of reasoning. It may indicate that multiple meanings have not yet been separated.

Even the absence of intended meaning should not be forced into an explanation merely because an explanation can be constructed.

SIM therefore favors observation before evaluation, distinction before convergence, and explicit uncertainty before invented completeness.

## 3. Meta-Observationalism

The philosophical foundation of SIM is **Meta-Observationalism**.

Meta-Observationalism begins from the position that observation cannot simply be assumed to be neutral or transparent. What is observed and how it is described may depend on the observer's perspective, assumptions, prior knowledge, context, language, objectives, and method of observation.

Rather than attempting to stand outside all of these influences, Meta-Observationalism makes them available for observation as well.

The subject is observed.

The observer's perspective can also be observed.

The interpretation produced by the observer can also be observed.

Differences between observations can also be observed.

This does not imply endless skepticism, nor does it claim that every interpretation is equally valid. It establishes a discipline: interpretation should not silently acquire authority merely because it was produced during observation.

Meta-Observationalism is developed in **Vol. 1**.

## 4. Reflexive Observation Principle

From Meta-Observationalism, SIM derives the **Reflexive Observation Principle**.

The principle requires observation to be reflexive: the observer must be able to turn observation back toward the conditions and acts through which the observation was produced.

In practical terms, this means distinguishing, when relevant:

- what was observed,
- from which perspective it was observed,
- what assumptions or context influenced the observation,
- what interpretation was added,
- what remains unknown or unresolved,
- and where different observations diverge.

The purpose is not to remove the observer from reasoning. The observer is unavoidable and often valuable.

The purpose is to prevent the observer from becoming invisible.

The Reflexive Observation Principle is developed in **Vol. 2**.

## 5. From Principle to Method

SIM is the reasoning method derived from this philosophical position and principle.

Its concern is meaning.

During reasoning, different observations, terms, interpretations, assumptions, and contexts can become prematurely merged. Once merged, distinctions that were important to the original subject may disappear.

SIM attempts to keep those distinctions observable long enough for semantic boundaries to be discovered and stabilized.

A typical movement of SIM reasoning can be expressed as:

```text
Observation
    ↓
Difference
    ↓
Question
    ↓
Boundary Hypothesis
    ↓
Semantic Probe
    ↓
Re-observation
    ↓
Semantic Isolation
```

This is not intended as a mandatory linear workflow. Reasoning may move backward, repeat, branch, or remain unresolved.

The important property is that meaning is not forced to converge before the distinctions relevant to that meaning have been observed.

The domain-independent basics of the method are developed in **Vol. 3**.

## 6. AI as the Context of Discovery

SIM was discovered through work with AI, and AI remains an especially useful context in which to understand it.

AI systems can rapidly generate plausible interpretations, fill missing information, connect unrelated observations, normalize differences, and converge toward coherent narratives. These capabilities are often useful. They also make semantic collapse unusually easy to observe.

The problem, however, is not unique to AI.

Humans also interpret incomplete information, inherit assumptions, normalize differences, construct narratives, and confuse observation with evaluation.

AI therefore does not define SIM's scope.

AI was the context in which the need for the method became particularly visible.

For SIM, an AI can be treated as a powerful observer — but not as an inherently neutral observer, a source of truth, or semantic authority merely because it can produce a convincing answer.

Guidance for AI reasoning is developed in **Vol. 4**.

## 7. SIM and Applied SIM

SIM itself is not a software-development process, business-analysis technique, system-analysis procedure, architecture method, research methodology, or consulting framework.

It is a generic semantic reasoning method.

Domain-specific methods may be derived from it. These are referred to as **Applied SIM**.

Applied SIM may define concrete workflows, artifacts, roles, prompts, checkpoints, or domain-specific rules. Such applications are useful, but they should not be confused with the foundational method from which they are derived.

This distinction is important because SIM historically appeared first through applied forms, particularly in AI-assisted software development.

The applications came first in time.

The foundation came first in logic.

## 8. Why Version 0

The documents in this foundational series are designated **Version 0**.

Version 0 does not mean that SIM is preliminary, unstable, experimental, or incomplete.

It indicates that these documents describe the layer logically prior to the applied versions through which SIM was first expressed.

The foundational philosophy and principles were not written first and then applied. They were discovered by examining the behavior, distinctions, and recurring constraints that had already emerged in applied SIM.

Version 0 is therefore an act of semantic isolation applied to SIM itself.

The method is being separated from its first applications so that its underlying meaning can be observed independently of them.

This also means that the canon is not exempt from its own principle.

SIM may itself be observed, questioned, compared, and re-examined. Canonical status identifies semantic authority for these documents; it does not make their contents immune to observation.

## 9. Position of the Canon

The canonical structure is:

```text
Meta-Observationalism
        ↓
Reflexive Observation Principle
        ↓
Semantic Isolation Method
        ↓
SIM Reasoning Guidelines for AI
        ↓
Applied SIM
```

These layers should not be collapsed into one another.

The philosophy explains the position from which observation is understood.

The principle defines the reflexive behavior expected of observation.

The method provides a general way to preserve and isolate semantic distinctions.

The AI guidelines apply the method to a particularly important class of observers.

Applied SIM introduces domain-specific procedures.

Together, the documents in this repository define the foundational layer from which those applications may be derived.

SIM begins with a simple constraint:

> **Do not assume that seeing something means seeing it without an observer.**

And from that constraint follows its central practice:

> **Observe the observer.**
